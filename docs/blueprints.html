Blueprints

Blueprints
Whole systems, as graphs

A blueprint is not a chart to install. It is a shape: which components, which links, which sub-components — and what each edge generates. Every one of these is assembled from the same catalog, and every one of them validates and attests the same way.

BlueprintMachine learning

Experiments, model registry, training jobs on GPU, promotion by alias, and two places where a human deliberately signs.

BlueprintPrivate AI

Self-hosted inference, a chat interface behind single sign-on, retrieval over your own documents, and an AI gateway in front of it all.

BlueprintDigital sovereignty

Collaboration, mail, documents, video, wiki and ERP on your own infrastructure, with one identity across all of it.

BlueprintMulti-environment SaaS

One application, several environments, real autoscaling, and a compliance position you can hand to a customer.

What they have in common​

Every blueprint below is a composition, not a special case. The platform does not have an "ML mode" or a "sovereignty mode" — it has components, links and sub-components, and these are arrangements of them.

That matters for two reasons. You can start from one and change it, because nothing about it is fixed. And everything the rest of this documentation describes — generation, validation, scanning, evidence, compliance, backup, lifecycle — applies to all of them without qualification.

Starting from one​

The fastest path is to describe what you want to your AI client and let it propose the composition from the real catalog. Blueprints are useful as a shared vocabulary for that conversation — "something like the private AI blueprint, but with our own retrieval corpus and no public exposure" is a complete brief.

The human position of record

Sign-off
An approval signed by a model is worthless to the auditor who reads it

Automation decides what the cluster proves. An audit also needs a human position on everything the script cannot pass. Both are kept, and they are never confused with each other.

Reviews on controls​

Controls found in place are approved by the script by default. Controls found not in place stay open until a person records one of the following:

StateMeaningEffect
gap_confirmedAcknowledged gap, with an owner and a planStill a gap, but owned
compensating_controlAnother control covers the riskAudit-defensible position
not_applicableWith justificationAudit-defensible position
risk_acceptedRequires an expiry dateAudit-defensible until that date
proposedA suggestion — the only state an AI may writeNo effect on counts
annotatedOn an in-place control: approval stands, context addedNone
rejectedOn an in-place control: the customer overrules the scriptCounted as a gap of record

The customer keeps the last word in both directions. A control the script passed can be rejected; a control it failed can be positioned.

A review is bound to what it was signed against​

Every review stores the run, the run hash, the automated status at the time, and a hash of the evidence. That is what makes a sign-off meaningful a year later.

It reopens when it expires, or when the control's automated status changes. When only the evidence text changed, it is flagged rather than reopened — so a reworded finding does not invalidate a year of positions, and a real change does.

Owner and reason are mandatory. Writes are gated by an explicit attribute and refused without durable storage, so a decision cannot be recorded somewhere it will be lost.

The same model on links​

Link validation uses the identical mechanism. A failing or partial link can be positioned exactly like a control; a proven link can be annotated or rejected. A positioned link stops counting as a regression in the timeline; a rejected pass becomes a finding.

This matters because a validation surface that cannot express "we know, and here is why it is acceptable" gets turned off.

Approval gates​

Reviews answer a question the system asked. A gate is the opposite direction: something is waiting on a human answer and will not start without it.

A database migration, a deploy, a restore over live data, a job that costs real money — all the same record. The gate stores the answer, never how the answer was reached. It deliberately knows nothing about what it is about, because the moment it grows conditional rules it becomes a policy language nobody understands a year later.

An AI may propose, and nothing else

This is enforced at the door, not by convention. A model can record a proposal on a control or a gate. It cannot approve one. The signature on an audit-defensible position is a person's.

The journal​

Every decision — recorded, changed, expired, revoked — is appended to a journal. Combined with the attestation chain on the runs themselves, that gives you the two halves an audit actually asks for: what the system was, and who said it was acceptable.

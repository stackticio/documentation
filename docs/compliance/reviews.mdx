## Reviews on controls

Controls found **in place** are approved by the script by default. Controls found **not in
place** stay open until a person records one of the following:

| State | Meaning | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `gap_confirmed` | Acknowledged gap, with an owner and a plan | Still a gap, but owned |
| `compensating_control` | Another control covers the risk | Audit-defensible position |
| `not_applicable` | With justification | Audit-defensible position |
| `risk_accepted` | **Requires an expiry date** | Audit-defensible until that date |
| `proposed` | A suggestion — the only state an AI may write | No effect on counts |
| `annotated` | On an in-place control: approval stands, context added | None |
| `rejected` | On an in-place control: the customer overrules the script | Counted as a gap of record |

The customer keeps the last word in both directions. A control the script passed can be
rejected; a control it failed can be positioned.

## A review is bound to what it was signed against

Every review stores the run, the run hash, the automated status at the time, and a hash of
the evidence. That is what makes a sign-off meaningful a year later.

It **reopens** when it expires, or when the control's automated status changes. When only
the evidence text changed, it is flagged rather than reopened — so a reworded finding does
not invalidate a year of positions, and a real change does.

Owner and reason are mandatory. Writes are gated by an explicit attribute and refused
without durable storage, so a decision cannot be recorded somewhere it will be lost.

## The same model on links

Link validation uses the identical mechanism. A failing or partial link can be positioned
exactly like a control; a proven link can be annotated or rejected. A positioned link stops
counting as a regression in the timeline; a rejected pass becomes a finding.

This matters because a validation surface that cannot express "we know, and here is why it
is acceptable" gets turned off.

## Approval gates

Reviews answer a question the system asked. A **gate** is the opposite direction:
something is waiting on a human answer and will not start without it.

A database migration, a deploy, a restore over live data, a job that costs real money — all
the same record. The gate stores the answer, never how the answer was reached. It
deliberately knows nothing about what it is about, because the moment it grows conditional
rules it becomes a policy language nobody understands a year later.

:::danger An AI may propose, and nothing else
This is enforced at the door, not by convention. A model can record a proposal on a control
or a gate. It cannot approve one. The signature on an audit-defensible position is a
person's.
:::

## The journal

Every decision — recorded, changed, expired, revoked — is appended to a journal. Combined
with the attestation chain on the runs themselves, that gives you the two halves an audit
actually asks for: what the system was, and who said it was acceptable.

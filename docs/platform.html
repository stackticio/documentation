The platform, screen by screen

The platform
The platform, screen by screen

The platform is the web application where you draw a system, configure it, build it into a Git repository and keep every version of it. This page walks through it in the order you meet it and says what each screen and button does.

The menu​

Menu entryWhat it is
SystemsYour systems and the ones shared with you, in folders
DesignThe canvas where the system is drawn. Most of this page is about it
ConfigurationThe system's Git repository, image registry, domain, environments and secret encryption
BuildGenerates the repository from the drawing, in full or for selected components
HistoryEvery save and every build, with a diff between any two and rollback
CollaboratorsWho has which role on the system, including roles that expire
ScansOpens the stack agent's dashboard for this system
Components, Attributes, Suggestions, Sections, Papers, TestsThe catalog: what systems are drawn from
Profile, Git access tokens, RegistryYour account, credentials for your own template repositories, and your organisation's systems

Design, Configuration, Build, History, Collaborators and Scans appear once a system is open. The administration pages (licences, catalog distribution to other instances, organisations) are for whoever operates the platform instance and play no part in designing a stack.

Systems​

Each system is a card showing the picture of its last save, its status and the date of its last change. On your own systems the card has three icons:

IconDoes
PencilRenames the system
BinDeletes it. Copies of its cells held by other systems are detached first
EyePublishes the system's cells. While the eye is open, its components are offered under External systems on the design page of everyone who has a role on it

Personal systems are the ones you are admin on. Shared systems are the ones someone gave you a role on. Folders organise both. A new system needs a title that is unique among your systems. It starts with a default configuration (images built with Kaniko, one environment called dev) and you as its admin.

A system's status is one of Created, Saved, Building, Generated or Failed.

Rename before you publish

Systems that hold copies of your cells find your system by its title. Renaming a published system silently breaks their link back to it.

Design​

The design page has four areas: the component panel on the left, the canvas in the middle, the toolbar above it, and the inspector on the right, which shows the selected cell.

The component panel​

The panel lists the catalog in groups such as Frontend, Backend, Data and Authentication. The groups and their order come from the catalog. The search box filters every group. Drag an entry onto the canvas to add that component.

The Draw group holds plain shapes, like rectangles and ellipses, for zones and notes. Boxes such as Platform ops or Security & policy are drawn with them. They are for drawing only: generation reads components, sub-components and links, and ignores everything else on the canvas.

The label at the top of the panel switches it to External systems. This lists the components of the published systems you have a role on. A system with a pending catalog change is left out until it is synchronized. When you drop one of these components:

  • a copy is added, named after its system: <system title>_<component name>;
  • its sub-components come with it;
  • its links to other copies already on your canvas are recreated.

You link your own components to a copy like to any other. Two copies from the same system cannot be linked to each other, because their links belong to the system they come from. What a copy generates is covered in Collaboration.

Drawing​

Select a component and handles appear around it:

HandleDoes
LinkDrag to another component to connect the two
ConnectionsLists the components on the canvas this one may link to; click one to create the link
Add sub-componentLists the sub-components the catalog lets this component own, such as a database, a bucket, a topic or a client
UnlinkRemoves every link of the component
RemoveDeletes the component
RotateRotates it

You can only draw a link where the catalog defines one between those two component types, and only once in each direction. The new link is named after both types, <source type>-<target type> (for example fastapi-db). That name is what the templates read. The link takes its default attributes from the catalog. It also takes the catalog's colour for that link type, which is why links on the canvas have different colours.

Right-click a component or a link for Copy, Paste and Guide. The guide is the catalog's documentation for that component type or link type, when it has one.

The usual shortcuts work: Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+X, Delete, Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y, Ctrl+A, Ctrl+plus, Ctrl+minus. Ctrl+S is Save & Render.

The inspector​

The inspector shows the selected component:

  • its name;
  • its catalog version;
  • the environments it is deployed to (dev by default);
  • its attributes, grouped as the catalog groups them: Information, Configuration, Deploy, Resources, Auto-Scaling, Security, Access Control, Keys & Access, Tuning and Unique attributes.

Each field has the type the catalog gives it: text, long text, toggle, single or multiple choice, code editor or password. A choice field offers only the catalog's options. The Design group sets the component's colours and image.

Two fields behave differently:

  • Open Helm Editor appears on components installed from a Helm chart. It renders the component's Helm values from the canvas as it is now, unsaved changes included, and marks the keys Stacktic sets from the design. You can add values. The editor refuses a change to a marked key and a duplicate key.
  • External systems appears on a published component and lists the systems that hold a copy of it.

The toolbar​

ButtonWhat it does
Save & RenderStores the drawing as a new version (a row in History) and copies your changes into systems that hold copies of your cells. Then, in the background, it validates the drawing against the catalog and renders the components whose configuration changed. Moving cells without changing anything else renders nothing. If validation fails, the errors appear on that save's row in History and nothing is rendered
Save & PushSaves, then regenerates only the files whose templates read an attribute you changed since the last push, and commits them. The log lists the kubectl apply commands for exactly those files. It refuses when you added or removed a component, sub-component or link; run a Build for that. It commits nothing if the merge into your branch conflicts. A push leaves no row in History
Undo, RedoUndo and redo changes on the canvas
Clear Paper (bin icon)Empties the canvas. Nothing is stored until you save
SVGOpens the drawing as an SVG image
Export, ImportExport downloads the drawing as system.json. Import replaces the canvas with one and drops any component that is not in the catalog
Send To Front, Send To BackChange which overlapping cell is on top
SectionsSystem-wide values, and a set of attributes edited across every component at once. See Catalog and sections
SynchronizeAppears, in yellow, only when the catalog changed a component type this design uses. Both save buttons stay disabled until you click it. It brings the attributes, links and sub-components of those cells up to the new catalog definition, and removes cells whose type was deleted
Zoom to fit, zoom sliderFit the drawing to the window; zoom in and out
Links VisibilityHides or shows the links of each component. It only changes the display, never what is generated
Show suggestionsMakes components that match a catalog suggestion blink; click one to read the suggestion. Suggestions are evaluated each time you save with the box ticked
Switch PaperOpens the drawing as a Rationale paper or a Test paper, described next

Both save buttons check one rule in the browser before saving: two components may not share a namespace. Apart from that, catalog validation never blocks a save on this page. It runs in the background after Save & Render, is listed on the build page, and is enforced by the API.

Papers: more layers on the same drawing​

Switch Paper lists the papers you can use, yours and Stacktic's, grouped by category. A paper shows the same drawing with another layer on it. Switch Paper again returns to the design.

PaperWhat you seeWhere it comes from
RationaleA Rationale field on every component, sub-component and link, for why it exists, what it carries and which rule of the application depends on it. Show Rationale Graph opens one cell with its links, its neighbours and their textsWritten by people or by an AI, stored in the drawing and saved with each version. Nothing generated reads it. See Knowledge
TestEach component coloured by the result of its tests against the running cluster: red for critical, amber for warning, green when nothing failed, grey when it has no testsThe tests the catalog attaches to each component type in that paper, run by the system's stack agent. See LiveView
DataListed, but opening one does nothing yet—

Configuration​

This page holds what the system needs to build and push. Every value on it reaches every template of the system. That is how one domain or one registry setting ends up in every manifest.

FieldPassed to templates asUsed for
Registry host, authentication URI, project, username, password, emailregistry_*Where images are pushed and pulled, and the pull secret
Git host, project, Azure repository, owner, branch, username, email, passwordgit_*The repository the build commits to. Test Connection checks it
Build namespace, build branchbuild_namespace, build_branchWhere the in-cluster image builds run, and the branch they read source from
Image builder: Kaniko or BuildKitbuild_typeWhich builder the image-build manifests use
DomaindomainEvery hostname the stack exposes is a subdomain of it
Environmentsthe environment listThe environments each component can be placed in. Every environment a component is in gets its own overlay folder
SOPS enabled, Age recipients, Age secret keysops_*Encrypting the secrets written to the repository

The system's title is passed as system_name. The configuration can be exported and imported. Admins, maintainers and security auditors can open this page.

Build​

The build page first lists the drawing's validation errors (component type, attribute, value and message), then offers four actions.

ActionWhat it does
BuildRenders every component from its template, runs the stack-wide generation steps and commits the result. If the system shares cells with other systems, you first choose which shared components are rendered into your repository (see What a copy generates). Then you choose the Stacktic version (the version of the stack-wide steps) and whether to merge into your branch
Fast BuildRenders only the components you tick, with their sub-components, then runs the stack-wide steps and commits. Components you did not tick keep the output of the last build, even when they read something you changed. The page warns about this
PushCommits the whole system from what the last Save & Render already rendered, plus the stack-wide steps. It is a full build without waiting for rendering. It is refused until that background render has finished
Cancel BuildStops a running build between steps and between components

Merging into your branch. Stacktic commits to its own branch and merges it into yours. With merge on, the build lists every file in your repository that differs from what Stacktic would write. For each file you choose:

  • merge, the default;
  • overwrite, to take Stacktic's version;
  • ignore, to leave the file alone from now on.

Ignore choices are remembered for later builds and pushes. A build that still conflicts commits the conflict markers and lists the files in its log. A push that conflicts commits nothing.

The build log streams while the build runs and stays on the build's row in History.

History​

There is one row per save and per build. Each row shows the ID, message, status, whether it merged, the Stacktic version, who made it and when.

  • Compare shows the difference between two versions, cell by cell.
  • Rollback loads a version's drawing onto the canvas without saving it. It becomes the current version when you save.

Pushes are not recorded here. See History and rollback.

Collaborators​

RoleCan
AdminEverything, including managing collaborators
MaintainDesign, build, push, configuration
WriteDesign, build, push
Security auditorRead the system and open its configuration
ReadSee the system

A role can be given until a date (UTC), with a reason. When that date passes, the person goes back to the role they had before. Roles history keeps every grant, change, expiry and revocation. See Collaboration.

Scans​

Scans opens the stack agent's dashboard (scans, scores, evidence) in a new tab at <subdomain>.<domain>/ui/. The subdomain is the one set on the link from the API gateway (APISIX) to the stack agent. Until the design has that link, the menu entry says so instead of opening. See The stack agent.

The catalog​

PageHolds
ComponentsOne entry per component type and version, holding:
  • its technical ID, which is the name of its template folder;
  • label, description, family (its group in the component panel) and logo;
  • its attributes, with defaults and allowed values;
  • the sub-components it can own;
  • the links it can have to and from other types, each with attributes, a colour and a guide;
  • its guides;
  • the Git repository and branch its template lives in.
One version of each type is the default
AttributesAttribute definitions: technical ID (the name templates read), label, group, type, allowed options and a validation pattern
SuggestionsRules checked against a drawing: a required component, a link to or from a type, or an attribute value. A match puts a suggestion on the component
SectionsSystem-wide attributes, plus a chosen set of component attributes to edit across a design from one tab
PapersRationale, Test and Data papers, yours and Stacktic's
TestsFor each component type and version, the tests in each Test paper. A test has a command, a target, a filter, a severity, messages and a recommendation (below)

Test targets are what a test checks:

  • the component itself;
  • its sub-components;
  • its links to other components;
  • its links from other components;
  • its sub-components' links;
  • its attributes.

A test's severity is info, warning or critical. It has one message for success and one for failure, plus a recommendation.

Changing a component type in the catalog notifies every system that uses it. Those designs show Synchronize until they take the change.

A component type of your own can come with a template that Stacktic writes for you. With auto source code or auto Helm set, saving the type writes a starter template into its Git repository and pushes it. See Your own components.

Your account​

  • Profile: your name and email.
  • Git access tokens: username and password pairs for the Git repositories that hold your own component templates. A catalog component points at one of them.
  • Registry: your organisation's systems, with their owner and who they are shared with.

What happens when you build​

Each component is rendered from its template. The template receives:

  • the component and its attributes;
  • its links in both directions, with the component at the other end of each;
  • its sub-components;
  • every other component of the system;
  • the configuration values above.

Then the stack-wide steps run, in this order:

StepRuns when
Write stack-metadata.yaml, the machine-readable description of the whole graphAlways
Generate network policies from the linksUnless the section value microsegmentation_level is none. The value pci_dss_strict generates the strict set
Add content checksums, so that a changed secret or configuration restarts its podsWhen checksum_annotations is true
List every image and rewrite the manifests to pull from your registryWhen air_gapped is true

The output of all components is then merged into one repository tree and committed. The tree has k8s/build, k8s/deploy (one overlay per environment), doc and scripts. See From graph to repository.

<Hero eyebrow="Guide" title="The graph knows what; Knowledge says why">
A drawing says that a topic feeds a database. It does not say what the data is, why that
sink was chosen, or which business rule depends on it. Knowledge is that text, written
on the cell itself, kept with the design, and shown as its own page of the diagram.
</Hero>

## Papers

A **paper** is a named sheet in the catalog. It has a category: **Rationale** papers hold
knowledge, **Test** papers hold validation tests, **Data** papers are reserved. The Papers
page under the catalog lists yours next to the ones Stacktic ships, and lets you create one.
A paper named *Knowledge* is the usual place for the why of a stack; nothing stops you from
keeping a second one, say *Security*, for a different audience. When you create one, choose
**Rationale** — the dialog starts on *Data*, which nothing reads yet.

## Writing knowledge on the design page

<Steps>
  <Step title="Switch paper">
    On the design page, use <em>Switch Paper</em> and pick a Rationale paper. The diagram opens
    as that paper: same cells, same links, a different layer of text.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select a cell">
    A component, a sub-component or a link. Its inspector gains a <em>Rationale</em> field.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write and save">
    The Rationale Editor holds the text for that cell and that paper. Save the design as usual:
    the text is stored with the cell and travels with every version of the design.
  </Step>
</Steps>

With <em>Show Rationale Graph</em> on, clicking a cell opens the Rationale Graph: the cell's
text in the middle, every inbound and outbound link with its own text, the neighbouring cells,
and the sub-components a component contains. It is the quickest way to read a topology as a
story rather than as a drawing.

## Through the API and the MCP

The same text can be written by automation: an agent that has read your repository can fill
the Knowledge paper of a whole stack, cell by cell, and the design page shows it the next
time it opens. The platform API lists the papers and attaches a rationale to one cell of a
system without touching anything else in it; the MCP exposes it as one tool that lists,
reads and writes. Writing knowledge never triggers a build, a validation or a re-render,
and it needs the Admin, Maintain or Write role on the system.

Three facts to know when you automate it:

- **Cells are addressed by their label.** Two cells with the same label, for instance an
  application and the identity client named after it, cannot both be reached; keep labels
  unique on a stack you plan to document this way.
- **The text is shown as HTML.** Paragraph and line-break tags render; a bare newline does not.
- **Keep credentials out of it.** Knowledge is read by people and by AI tools.

## What it is not

Knowledge is documentation, not configuration. No template reads it, no policy is derived from
it, and no test runs from it. It is also stored in the graph rather than the catalog, so it
belongs to one system: the same component type on another stack starts with an empty page.

Nor is it the MCP's [knowledge layer](/docs/mcp/knowledge) — the platform knowledge an AI
client reads to design correctly. This page holds the why of *your* design; that one holds
what the platform can express.

:::caution
A single-attribute update made through the API rewrites the graph through a typed model that
does not carry rationales. Change attributes from the design page, or through the MCP, which
detects a documented stack and writes through the full graph instead. A design page left open
while automation writes knowledge will overwrite it on its next save; reload it first.
:::

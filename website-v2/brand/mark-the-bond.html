Three courses. The joints of the top and bottom rows fall in the middle — and one block spans them both. That block is the only thing holding the two halves together, and it is the shared TAC: the piece STACK and TACTIC have in common.

Why it is the right mark

It is the oldest engineering idea there is

This is running bond — the masonry rule that every course must be offset so the joints never stack. It is the reason a wall of loose bricks becomes one structure. Four thousand years old, and it is exactly your pitch: separate layers, bonded, become one system.

The lit block is the product

Everything else is a layer — anyone sells you layers. The mint block is the one that crosses the seam. That is what Stacktic is: not another component in the stack, the thing that bonds the components into a stack.

Nobody can copy it

An arch, a lock, a shield, a node graph — any infrastructure company can use those. This figure is generated by the letters of your own name. It is unownable by anyone else, which is the only real definition of a unique mark.

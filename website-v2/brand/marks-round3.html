Six marks. No stray K.

The K belongs inside the word, so nothing here is a letter standing in front of it. Each of these is an abstract mark that sits left of the wordmark without pretending to be a character. All shown at 96 / 32 / 24 / 16px, because 16px is what kills marks.

The six

A  Switchback

One unbroken line threading three layers and exiting. It is also an S. The route through the stack — literally a tactic.

B  Thrust

Three layers, each offset further right, the top one running on into an arrow. The whole stack moving as one.

C  Convergence

Every layer collapses into one point, and one truth leaves it. Source of truth, stated with nothing else in the frame.

D  Pinwheel

Four layers locked into a closed square — no loose ends, nothing outside the system. Rotational, so it works at any angle.

E  Knight

Tactic is a chess word. The knight is the only piece that jumps layers — two up, one across, over a 3×3 board.

F  Custody

Layers held inside two brackets. Everything between these is governed — the compliance claim, drawn.

My read: A and B are the two with real legs.
A · Switchback — one line, three layers, one exit. It is also an S, so the mark and the initial are the same object. That is the kind of coincidence you build a brand on.
B · Thrust — the boldest at 16px and the fastest to read: the stack, moving.
D is handsome but says nothing specific. E is the most original idea on the page and the biggest risk — chess may read as a games company. F is the clearest compliance statement and the least distinctive shape.

Icons come next, not now. Once you pick, the whole icon set gets built from that mark's geometry — switchback icons look nothing like thrust icons. Building 16 before the mark is settled is what I got wrong last round.
